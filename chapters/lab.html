BCD Process Lab

A power IC cross-section emerges from linked process modules: wells and isolation, drift and body implants, gate and field structures, source/drain formation, contacts, and metal. Use the editable sandbox to see how simplified process steps change a material and dopant map, then connect those steps to an annotated illustrative LDMOS structure. The examples are educational models, not foundry recipes.

  • Relate BCD device regions to the process modules that form them.
  • Compare how mask placement, implant conditions, deposition, etch, and anneal alter an educational cross-section.
  • Use an illustrative HV nLDMOS module sequence while distinguishing dopant visualization from electrical-field or breakdown simulation.

What the process model represents

The sandbox divides a small two-dimensional region into square cells. Each cell stores one material and a simplified dopant concentration; each process step applies an approximate rule to that grid.

ModuleEducational modelExample controls
EtchApproximate directional ion and isotropic radical contributions remove exposed cells according to material selectivity. Wet etch is represented as isotropic.Chemistry, rate, time, anisotropy, angle spread, tilt
DepositionSurface growth depends on visibility and a sticking factor. Conformal and directional limits illustrate step coverage and possible pinch-off.Material, method, thickness, sticking factor, directionality
LithographyA simplified one-dimensional aerial image, resist absorption, standing-wave, bake, and development model creates an opening or retained line.Tool model, pattern, width, dose, focus, thickness
ImplantA Gaussian projected-range profile is placed in exposed silicon using a simplified stopping-depth approximation; lateral spread is approximate.Species, energy, dose, tilt, channeling tail
Anneal / oxidationDiffusion is represented by a simplified thermal length; oxidation advances a local interface using an educational Deal–Grove approximation.Temperature, time, ambient
CMPCells above a stop surface or target height are removed, with an optional simplified dishing term.Stop material, over-polish, dishing
Model boundary

This retained cell-grid engine is for qualitative process intuition. It is not process TCAD: it does not model a complete BCD recipe, calibrated implant activation, three-dimensional layout, electric-field crowding, breakdown voltage, or device performance. Grid stair-steps and edge artifacts are expected. All values in the presets are illustrative assumptions.

Editable process sandbox

Choose a module and conditions in the control panel, then select Add step to update the material and dopant map. Select an earlier step to inspect that state; remove a step to discard it and later steps. Editing from an earlier state replaces the subsequent history.

Experiments to try

  1. Drift-mask placement. In the BCD nLDMOS preset, change the opening width or implant energy and compare the dopant footprint. This grid does not translate the profile into breakdown voltage.
  2. Thermal budget. Change the activation temperature or time in the nLDMOS sequence. The diffusion response is a simplified trend, not a calibrated implant-activation model.
  3. Isolation and field structure. Compare the STI and LOCOS templates, then read BCD architecture and HV device explanations to connect isolation to well boundaries and field management.
  4. Contact fill. Increase the deposition sticking factor in the contact-void template. Observe how a directional or high-sticking model can pinch off a narrow opening.

Key points

  1. BCD devices combine linked modules for wells, isolation, drift regions, gates, junctions, contacts, and metal.
  2. Mask placement, etch selectivity, deposition coverage, implant, and thermal history all affect the resulting geometry or dopant map.
  3. The XS cell-grid sandbox explains selected process trends; it is not calibrated process TCAD or a BV/field solver.
  4. Use the annotated nLDMOS drawing and linked device chapters for structure and operating intuition; treat preset numbers as illustrative only.

Knowledge check

What does the BCD nLDMOS preset let you inspect?

The preset uses a cell-grid process model to illustrate selected masked implants and anneal. It does not calculate electric fields, BV, or foundry-specific structures.

In the simplified isotropic-etch model, what tends to happen when the isotropic contribution increases?

An isotropic component removes material laterally as well as vertically, so an exposed feature may undercut its mask. The model is qualitative.

The idealized diffusion length scales as √(Dt), so a fourfold time increase doubles the length when D is held constant. Real activation and diffusion are more complex.
