Characterization and Production Test

A PMIC must be characterized across operating conditions, not judged by a single nominal reading. This chapter connects DC regulation, load and line response, quiescent current, transient behavior, efficiency, switching waveforms, wafer-level monitors and production screening. The retained process-monitor examples show how measurements inform engineering decisions; product limits and qualification procedures come from the applicable specification and public standard.

  • Relate DC tests to line/load regulation, dropout, quiescent current and current limit.
  • Interpret transient and switching measurements alongside efficiency and thermal limits.
  • Connect wafer-level process-control monitors and electrical die sort to production screening.
  • Use optical fitting, overlay and SPC as supporting process-monitoring tools.
  • Recognize what simple capability and defect-yield models can and cannot establish.

Build a useful test matrix

For each measurement, record stimulus, load, input range, temperature, operating mode, instrument bandwidth and acceptance criterion. A load-step waveform is meaningful only when step magnitude and edge rate are stated; efficiency requires input and output power measurements at the same operating point. These are general characterization practices, and product limits remain product-specific. See the public TI LDO basics application report for LDO operating concepts and the NIST semiconductor characterization publication record for public metrology context.

Overlay and process control

Overlay is the relative alignment error between patterns on different layers. Wafer-level measurements can contain systematic components, such as translation, rotation and magnification, as well as random residual error. The following linear model illustrates those components; its ranges are not product specifications.

$$\Delta x = T_x + M_x\,x - R\,y + \varepsilon_x, \qquad \Delta y = T_y + M_y\,y + R\,x + \varepsilon_y$$
\(T\) is translation, \(M\) is magnification (ppm), and \(R\) is rotation (µrad). The simulator estimates a linear fit and shows its residual when correction is enabled. Real process control may also account for higher-order effects; this example is not a scanner correction recipe.

SPC: monitoring measurements over time

Statistical process control (SPC) tracks measurements over time. Control limits summarize process variation in the selected chart, while specification limits (USL and LSL) come from product requirements. A control-chart signal prompts investigation; it does not by itself identify a cause. Capability indices compare process spread and centering with the specification window.

$$C_p = \frac{\mathrm{USL}-\mathrm{LSL}}{6\sigma}, \qquad C_{pk} = \frac{\min(\mathrm{USL}-\mu,\ \mu-\mathrm{LSL})}{3\sigma}$$
These indices summarize a stable process under distributional assumptions. A capability value is not a guarantee of field failure rate, and a meaningful estimate requires suitable data, sampling and specification limits.

Defect screening and yield context

Defect maps and electrical sort data provide different views of manufacturing outcomes. A defect-density yield model is a simplified way to explore how defect density, die area and clustering can affect expected yield. It is not a prediction for a specific PMIC process or product.

$$Y_{\text{Poisson}} = e^{-AD_0}, \quad Y_{\text{Murphy}} = \left(\frac{1-e^{-AD_0}}{AD_0}\right)^2, \quad Y_{\text{NB}} = \left(1+\frac{AD_0}{\alpha}\right)^{-\alpha}$$
In the negative-binomial model, \(\alpha\) controls clustering: as \(\alpha\to\infty\), the expression approaches Poisson; smaller values represent stronger clustering in this model. These formulas illustrate assumptions and do not replace observed electrical yield data.

Key takeaways

  1. Specify input, load, operating mode, temperature and measurement setup alongside every electrical result.
  2. Use DC, transient, switching and efficiency results together; one nominal point cannot characterize a PMIC rail.
  3. WAT/PCM monitors process structures, while EDS/wafer sort screens individual dies before assembly.
  4. SPC and guard bands help interpret variation; capability indices rely on assumptions and do not replace product limits.
  5. Production-test coverage balances relevant defect screening, repeatability and test time.

Knowledge check

A regulator output changes as input voltage is swept while load is held constant. Which metric is being evaluated?

Line regulation describes output change as input voltage changes with load and other conditions controlled.

Which capture is most useful for measuring recovery after a sudden increase in load current?

A controlled load-step waveform reveals undershoot, overshoot, ringing and recovery time.

What is the role of EDS or wafer sort in a production flow?

Wafer sort probes each die and bins its electrical results against defined product limits before assembly.
