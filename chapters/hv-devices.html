HV MOS and LDMOS devices

A low-voltage MOSFET places a short channel between source and drain. A high-voltage lateral DMOS (LDMOS) adds a lateral drift region so much of the off-state voltage can be supported away from the channel. That extra region takes silicon area and contributes resistance. The details depend on device architecture and process; an IEEE publication discusses LDMOS/RESURF structures, but its particular results should not be generalized to all platforms (IEEE Xplore document 9813626).

  • Locate the channel, drift region, and field-crowding locations.
  • Explain the qualitative breakdown-voltage / on-resistance tradeoff.
  • Understand RESURF as a field-distribution design idea, not a universal formula.

Where the off-state voltage is supported

In the off state, depletion extends into the drift region and spreads the voltage drop. Abrupt geometry and material interfaces can concentrate electric field (“field crowding”), often making the drain-side surface or junction a critical location. Field plates, graded doping, and charge balance are among the design tools used to shape that field.

From depletion to a two-dimensional field problem

The starting equation is Poisson's law, not a universal LDMOS breakdown formula. In silicon, \(\nabla\!\cdot(\varepsilon_{Si}\nabla\psi)=-\rho\), \(\mathbf E=-\nabla\psi\), and \(\rho=q(p-n+N_D^+-N_A^-)\) before trapped/interface charge is added at boundaries. In an ideal fully depleted n-type region with uniform ionized donors, \(\rho\approx qN_D\). For a one-dimensional abrupt planar junction with \(N_A\gg N_D\), the depletion width lies mostly in the n side, so \(W\approx x_n\), \(|E_{max}|\approx qN_DW/\varepsilon_{Si}\), and \(V_{dep}\equiv V_{bi}+V_R\approx qN_DW^2/(2\varepsilon_{Si})\). Here \(W\) is the n-side depletion width in m, \(N_D\) is in m−3, and \(\varepsilon_{Si}\) is in F/m; a comparable p-side doping needs the two-sided junction equations. MIT OCW Lecture 13, pp. 7–9 derives this depletion construction. An nLDMOS also has lateral and vertical depletion, curved junctions, oxide and plate boundaries, and a gate/body junction, so the planar result is a dimensional check, not a BV prediction.

RESURF engineering asks whether the integrated depleted drift charge can be balanced by adjacent p-type charge while voltage is distributed laterally. A useful bookkeeping quantity is \(Q_{D,area}=q\int_0^t N_D(z)\,dz\) [C/m²]. Equality of two sheet-charge magnitudes is only a necessary idealized charge-balance picture: field peaks still depend on lateral profiles, junction curvature, oxide charge, and terminal biases. Hower and Pendharkar, IRPS 2005, p. 2, Fig. 3 describe the n-drift/p-layer charge balance and the resistance price of lengthening the extension. Their examples are particular power IC devices.

$$\bar E_x=\frac{\Delta V}{L_d};\quad 12\,\mathrm V/6\,\mu\mathrm m=2\,\mathrm{V/\mu m},\qquad N_Dt=(10^{16}\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}})(0.5\,\mu\mathrm m)=5\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}$$
Worked assumptions: uniform drift, 12 V dropped over 6 µm, donor concentration 10¹⁶ cm⁻³, and thickness 0.5 µm. Mean lateral field and donor sheet count do not reveal the peak field, ionization integral, or breakdown voltage. The numbers are not a PMIC process specification.

Breakdown versus specific on-resistance

When on, current flows through the channel and drift region. A longer or more lightly doped drift region can support more voltage before avalanche, but it also adds resistance. A useful comparison is the specific on-resistance \(R_{on,sp}=R_{on}A\), usually reported in Ω·area (often mΩ·mm²). It normalizes resistance by active area but omits termination, contacts, metal, and temperature unless those are included in the measurement definition.

$$R_{on}\simeq\left.\frac{V_{DS}}{I_D}\right|_{\text{small }V_{DS}}\ [\Omega],\qquad R_{on,sp}=R_{on}A_{footprint}\ [\Omega\,\mathrm{m}^2]$$
Use the low-VDS slope or stated test point, gate bias, temperature, and a disclosed area definition. The measured value may include channel, accumulation, drift, source/contact, and metal contributions.

RESURF (“reduced surface field”) broadly describes approaches that use charge distribution and depletion to reduce peak surface field and use more of the drift region to support voltage. It is an intuition for field management, not a guarantee that one named structure will meet a specified breakdown voltage. Refer to the BCD architecture chapter for where these devices sit alongside low-voltage and bipolar regions.

In a deliberately restricted uniform, neutral drift slab, conductivity is \(\sigma=q\mu_nN_D\) [S/cm] and \(R_{drift}\approx L_d/(q\mu_nN_DtW_f)\). Taking the above assumed \(L_d=6\,\mu\mathrm m\), \(t=0.5\,\mu\mathrm m\), \(N_D=10^{16}\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}}\), an assumed \(\mu_n=800\,\mathrm{cm^2/(V\,s)}\), and total finger width \(W_f=1\,\mathrm{cm}\) yields \(R_{drift}\approx9.4\,\Omega\). This is only the uniform-slab term: depletion, nonuniform current density, high-field/temperature-dependent mobility, channel and contacts can change the result strongly. Doubling nominal doping would halve this term only if mobility and conducting area stayed fixed; it also changes off-state charge balance. The conductance relation follows MIT OCW Lecture 7.

Leakage, switching, and safe operating area

ConditionPhysical questionRequired evidence
Off stateDoes drift depletion hold voltage without excessive junction/surface leakage or avalanche multiplication?Specify VGS, VDS, T, leakage criterion, and a measured BV endpoint. A leakage rise alone does not identify its mechanism.
On stateWhere do channel, drift, contacts, and metal dissipate \(I_D^2R\), and is current crowded near a corner or finger?Low-VDS resistance versus gate bias and temperature; current/thermal mapping for the actual layout.
Fast switchingHow do nonlinear \(Q_g\), \(Q_{gd}\), output charge, driver impedance, and loop inductance affect transition energy and overshoot?Measured charge/voltage curves and switching waveforms at the specified bus/current. A single capacitance at one bias is insufficient. See Infineon gate-drive note, §§2–3.
Pulse or faultCan impact-generated holes raise p-body potential enough to forward-bias the source/body parasitic NPN, or can self-heating trigger instability?Body tie and pulse-duration-specific electrical/thermal SOA. Hower and Pendharkar, pp. 2–4, Figs. 7–10 separate snapback and thermal limits for their devices.

The n+ source–p-body–n-type drain path can act as a parasitic bipolar transistor under high-field injection. A lower-resistance p+ body connection helps remove generated holes in some architectures; it is not a universal guarantee against snapback or ESD failure. Power-induced temperature rise depends on pulse width and die/package/board heat paths. These coupled mechanisms require device/layout measurements and calibrated electrothermal analysis, not the geometry stepper in Chapter 24.

PMIC implications

  • Use blocking voltage with margin for transients and operating conditions; breakdown is not a recommended repetitive operating point.
  • Lower drift resistance can reduce conduction loss but may increase area, leakage, capacitance, or reduce voltage margin.
  • Switching loss also depends on gate charge, output capacitance, transition time, and driver strength.
  • Junction temperature changes resistance and reliability; characterization must match use conditions.

For a converter stage, connect these device limits to the switching chapter, current-path and thermal layout, and illustrative process integration.

Key points

  1. The drift region supports off-state voltage and adds on-state resistance.
  2. Field crowding can cause early breakdown; field shaping and charge design affect the result.
  3. RESURF is a family of field-management ideas; no simple universal equation predicts every LDMOS.
  4. Compare BV and \(R_{on,sp}\) only with test conditions and area definitions stated.

Knowledge check

Why does lengthening or lightly doping an LDMOS drift region often help voltage blocking but hurt conduction?

Voltage support and current conduction use the same drift region, creating a central geometry/doping tradeoff.

Does a 12 V drop across a 6 µm drift region prove a 2 V/µm peak field or a safe breakdown margin?

Poisson's equation and the boundary conditions are needed to find the spatial field; an average cannot establish an avalanche endpoint.

Why is a low-resistance p+ body tie relevant during a high-current nLDMOS pulse?

Impact-generated holes and body resistance can forward-bias the parasitic NPN; electrical and thermal SOA still require measurement.
