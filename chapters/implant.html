Chapter 14 · Doping profiles

Ion implantation for BCD

Ion implantation controls the dose and depth of dopants used to form wells, threshold-adjust regions, drift regions and source/drain junctions. For BCD devices, these profiles help set isolation, breakdown, on-resistance and parasitic behavior. Implantation also damages the crystal, so the profile must be understood together with annealing, activation and thermal budget.

  • Relate ion energy and species to projected range and profile width.
  • Explain dose, peak concentration, junction depth and mask stopping.
  • Recognize channeling, lateral spread, mask shadowing and implant damage.
  • Connect well and drift implants to BCD device structures without implying a foundry recipe.

Ion beam and dose control

An implanter selects an ion species, accelerates it to a chosen energy, scans the beam and measures integrated charge to estimate dose. Energy mainly sets the depth scale, while dose mainly sets the amount introduced. Species and molecular ions such as BF₂ also affect how energy is shared among atoms.

In BCD, different masks and implant conditions form wells and high-voltage drift regions as well as low-voltage junctions. The exact sequence and dose-energy windows are platform-specific; this chapter focuses on profile intuition.

Projected range and straggle

As an ion enters silicon, nuclear collisions redirect it and displace lattice atoms; electronic interactions also remove energy. Many ion trajectories produce a distribution described approximately by projected range \(R_p\) and straggle \(\Delta R_p\). A Gaussian is a first-order picture, not a complete implant simulation.

$$N(x)\approx\frac{\Phi}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Delta R_p}e^{-(x-R_p)^2/(2\Delta R_p^2)},\qquad N_p\approx\frac{0.4\Phi}{\Delta R_p}$$
Here \(\Phi\) is dose (cm⁻²). A metallurgical junction estimate requires a background concentration and active dopant profile; chemical dose alone is not enough.

Mask stopping and leakage

Resist, oxide, nitride or a gate can block an implant outside the intended region. Stopping depends on ion species, energy and mask composition. A Gaussian tail estimate can help frame a required thickness, but actual mask-edge transmission and secondary effects need process validation.

Tilt, channeling and lateral placement

Crystallographic channels can let ions penetrate deeper than a random-direction estimate. A small wafer tilt often reduces channeling, while tall masks can cast a geometric shadow. Lateral straggle and later diffusion also move dopants under mask edges. These effects are important for narrow isolation boundaries, well edges and high-voltage termination layouts.

Implant damage and annealing

Implants displace atoms and can amorphize a near-surface region at sufficiently high damage density. Annealing repairs some lattice disorder and promotes electrical activation, but can also cause diffusion or defect-related transient effects. Therefore an implant profile is not the final electrical profile.

See Annealing, activation and diffusion for thermal-history effects. For high-voltage BCD, drift-region dose and geometry trade breakdown against resistance; consult the high-voltage device lesson for that device-level connection.

Key points

  1. Implant energy sets a depth scale; dose sets the total introduced dopant amount.
  2. Projected range and straggle summarize a profile but omit channeling and detailed collision physics.
  3. Mask stopping, lateral straggle and tilt affect well, drift and junction boundaries.
  4. Implant damage requires anneal; active concentration and final junction depth depend on the full thermal sequence.
  5. All numerical values here are public educational approximations, not foundry-specific parameters.

Knowledge check

In a first-order implant model, what primarily sets the projected depth scale?

Species and energy determine stopping behavior and therefore the range distribution.

Why is a Gaussian implant profile only an approximation?

A complete profile depends on the collision cascade and process geometry.

Why does implantation need to be considered together with annealing?

The post-implant thermal sequence helps determine the final electrical profile.
